### The definitional anchor is in my own lineage, and it settles this

`harness.breaker.trigger_count` is defined as *"Consecutive failures that tripped the breaker"* — and its **Source column names `c9-reliability-recovery` SKILL.md (s12 §7.7)** (`Spec_Operational_Discipline_v1_32.md:41`; §7.1 last substantively defined at the v1.32 delta, OD head v1.36). C-CP-03 §3.4 keys the breaker to the `{provider, model}` pair (`Spec_Control_Plane_v1_2.md:389-391`, verified byte-exact this session; §3.4 last substantively defined at v1.2 per the delta-baseline convention). So the definitional silence the fork found at §1 is silence **in C9's own contract** — this is mine to answer, and the answer is already latent in Pillar 4's *purpose* clause: the per-model breaker exists to **"detect model-instance degradation"**; the per-provider breaker to **"detect provider outage."** [HIGH]

I state the counter-reading honestly: Pillar 4's *trigger* text ("N consecutive non-transient or budget-exhausted failures") is literally broad enough to admit reading (I). The disambiguator is the two words after it — **"from the same model."** A candidate-independent deterministic fault is not *from* any model; it is from the harness, observed while a model happened to be addressed. Counting it does not make the detector conservative, it makes the detector **wrong about its own subject**. [HIGH]

### What a half-open probe would test — and the fact that changes the cost of (I)

The recovery question is decisive, and grounding it surfaced a finding the filing does not carry:

**`attempt_half_open()` has ZERO production call sites** (`retry_breaker.py:289`; sweep across `harness-{runtime,cp,od}/src` this session). `should_attempt()` is a pure state read with no clock (`:309-311`). The registry caches breakers identity-stable for the process (`:379-400`), constructed once at bootstrap stage 5 per §14.6 D6. **Therefore `OPEN` is an absorbing state for the process lifetime at HEAD** — the shipped breaker is a one-way latch, and OD v1.32 §7.1 line 46 already writes contract text for an `open → half_open` transition the runtime never performs. [HIGH]

Two consequences:

1. Probe C's harm is worse than the filing prices it. It is not "all providers open, then cool down" — it is **all candidates permanently disabled for the run**, from one unset `RuntimeMemoryContext.record_scope`. Reading (I) ratifies that.
2. Even with half-open correctly wired (which C9 owes regardless — see below), a half-open trial after an internal-fault trip **re-executes the same deterministic fault and re-opens immediately.** The probe tests the harness config, never the provider. A breaker whose recovery model assumes the fault is *time-varying* and *endpoint-local* cannot serve either function on a fault that is neither. That is a category error, not a tuning choice. [HIGH]

Aggravating: `BreakerScope.PER_PROVIDER` exists in the enum but **no per-provider breaker is constructed anywhere** (fork §2.1, re-verified — the only construction is `PER_MODEL` at `retry_breaker_fallback.py:662-665`). This is my own **FM-O** (single-breaker-scope) live at HEAD. It means the *only* provider-wide health signal an operator gets today is "every per-model breaker opened" — precisely the signal Probe C manufactures from a config bug. [HIGH]

### Refinements to §6 the reliability domain requires

1. **The guard predicate must be `_is_candidate_independent(exc)`, not `_is_deterministic_harness_internal(exc)`.** Determinism alone is the wrong test — #2 and #3 are deterministic too and must keep counting. Candidate-independence is the property that makes counting information-free. With the #5 correction the guard collapses to a two-type tuple (`MemoryToolExecutionInternalError`, + B-115's type later) — simpler than the priced version, ~6 src lines at `:1016-1028`, guard on `record_failure` ONLY. [HIGH]
2. **Do not write the witnesses against a half-open path.** `attempt_half_open()` is unreachable at HEAD; a witness asserting "internal fault does not open the breaker, so the half-open probe stays meaningful" would be asserting a path that does not run. Assert the reachable property: `fail_count == 0` on all chain breakers after the internal fault, and `state is CLOSED`, plus the Probe-B multi-provider assertion across **both** providers.
3. **Register the dead half-open latch as a separate C9 row — do not fold it into B-116.** OD §7.1 already contracts an `open → half_open` transition the runtime never performs; my Pillar 4 requires "half-open after `half_open_after_ms`; one trial call; close on success, re-open on failure." That is a real gap whose *cost* (I) silently depends on, but it is a distinct arc. Same for **FM-O** (no per-provider breaker constructed). Naming both here so ratifying B-116 is not mistaken for closing them. [HIGH]
4. **Telemetry — the C10 seam.** `_emit_breaker_transition` (`retry_breaker_fallback.py:1130-1136`) passes neither `permanent_fail_repeats`, `tool_id`, nor `model_version`, so the C9↔C10 gating signal is **vacuous at the emission site today** — my own **FM-Q**, live. [HIGH] This matters directionally: fail-fast maps to `PERMANENT_FAIL_EXIT` (`:1024-1027`), so the moment anyone wires `permanent_fail_repeats` (a natural follow-on), reading (I) would drive C10's `gate_combination` to gate a **healthy** model+tool combination off a harness config bug. Reading (II)-as-corrected makes that signal *more* meaningful, not less: #4/#6 never reach the call, and #5 — the one member that legitimately feeds it — is exactly the model+tool capability mismatch `tool_id` was minted for.
5. **If #5 keeps counting it must not trip with `cause=None`.** `_classify_breaker_cause` duck-types on `.status_code` (`:374-406`) and yields `None` for it. The only existing member of the closed four-value `BreakerCause` domain that fits is `capability_shortfall` — currently unpopulated and described at OD v1.32 §7.1 (`:50`) as a forward-compatible slot, but whose in-code docstring scopes it to "cross-family fallback-exhaustion, not a per-step dispatch failure." **I surface the collision; I do not resolve it — the attribute domain is C7/OD's.** Minting a new value or a third `BreakerScope` is out of scope for this leg and I am not asking for it.

That is thin but *correct-and-thin* beats *wrong-and-loud*, and building a parallel counter here would be the over-engineering my own §3.2 discipline forbids. Register it as a forward observability row; do not price it into B-116.

### Strongest argument against my position, stated fairly

*The breaker is the chain's last budget, and (II) removes it.* Under (II), a persistent harness-internal fault produces an unbounded sequence of full chain traversals — every dispatch walks all N candidates, calls the provider path, fails identically, and exhausts, forever, with **nothing** accumulating state to stop it. Reading (I) at least converges: the breakers open, the chain short-circuits at the pre-check (`:674`), and the run degrades loudly and cheaply instead of grinding. On a metered provider that difference is real money and real latency, and "the operator will read the typed error" is exactly the kind of hand-wave that has failed before. This is the honest form of my own under-counting failure mode, and I do not think (II) fully answers it.

My answer, and its limit: the fault classes at issue (#4, #6) fail **before** the provider call in the fail-fast branch, so the repeat cost is chain-traversal overhead, not N× paid inference — which shrinks the argument materially but does not erase it (chain traversal still costs wall-clock and log volume). What the argument actually establishes is that **a repeat-detector for candidate-independent faults is genuinely owed** — it just must not be the provider-model breaker, whose signal it corrupts and whose recovery model it cannot use. Recommendation (4)/(6) above is where that debt is registered rather than silently absorbed. [MODERATE — the cost argument would strengthen to decisive if a candidate-independent fault class were ever admitted to the fail-fast branch *after* a paid provider call; that shape does not exist at HEAD but is the trigger to revisit.]
