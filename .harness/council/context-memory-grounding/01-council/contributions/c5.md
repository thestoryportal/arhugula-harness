## C5 — Validation Contract Architect (CONSULTANT): does the context/memory layer have *gates*, or only *prose*?

### The frame: the harness wrote itself a verification gate for its product state, and runs its process state on prose

My anchor is the IS read/write contract pair at **`Spec_Information_Substrate_v1.md` (v1.3) §C-IS-07 §7.1 (C3-pole write) + §7.2 (C2-pole read)** — and, decisively for my lens, **§6.4 `verify_chain(ledger) -> ChainVerificationResult`**. §C-IS-07 is not just a tension resolution; it is a *contract with a verification surface*. §7.1 commits append-only / structured / idempotent / hash-chain-preserving; §7.2 commits selective / bounded / navigation-mediated; and §6.4 specifies the **deterministic gate that checks conformance** — re-canonicalize, re-hash, compare, fail at the tamper position. That is the textbook C5 shape: a structural check wrapped into a deterministic-from-the-harness pass/fail decision the harness routes on, with tamper-evidence as the fail mode (§6.5). The harness *knows how to write a conformance gate over its own state.* It did so for `state.jsonl` (the H_T **product**).

The question my consultant seat owns is whether the harness applied that same discipline to its **process** state — the durable context/memory artifacts that govern *this* coding session: CLAUDE.md, MEMORY.md, the `.harness/` ledgers, checkpoints, and the future AGENTS/ARCHITECTURE/WORKFLOW/HOOKS docs. The answer is a sharp **three-tier gate-conformance map**, and the disease is concentrated in the gap between tiers. [HIGH — every tier verified against the actual `tools/` source this session]

### The three-tier gate-conformance map (the load-bearing C5 finding)

**Tier 1 — GATED (deterministic gate-as-contract; routes on pass/fail).** Exactly one process artifact is governed by a true C5-shaped gate:
- **`tools/substitution_ledger.py --check`** — `validate()` enforces both *per-row well-formedness* (duplicate-id, `disposition ∈ VALID_DISPOSITIONS`, `axis ∈ VALID_AXES`, `canonical` must be an explicit bool, the `label≠count` invariant) **and** *structural invariants* (the 54-canonical-row count, the bucket-sum identity, the 48-vs-49 pipeline-advanced impossibility class, and the **snapshot pin** — the only guard against a silent disposition flip). Exit 1 on any violation; CI-wired; has a contract test (`test_substitution_ledger.py`). **This is the model.** It is C3's data ledger given a C5 conformance gate — and it is, per C3's own words this session, "the one place the methodology is correctly applied."
- The supporting deterministic gates: **`stop-gate.sh`** (fast-lint Stop gate; `decision: block`; exit-code-mapped so a runner that *cannot run* surfaces a visible block, not a silent pass — a genuinely well-built gate with the exit-once loop-guard) and the **X-AL-3 CI guard** (file-presence gate over `design-substrate/**` edits).

**Tier 2 — ADVISORY-ONLY (gate-*shaped* check that routes on NOTHING).** This is where my lens fires hardest, because in my vocabulary these are *fail-class signals emitted without a pass/fail exit* — a gate that was checked but never wired:
- **The MEMORY.md byte-cap.** `session-end-cleanup.sh:53` detects `>24400B`, prints `OVER CAP, compact`, then `exit 0`. `loop-gc.sh:56` emits a `[hygiene]` advisory. **The harness observes the violation and proceeds anyway.** This is precisely why MEMORY.md is *27,051 bytes over a 24,400-byte cap and was reported "only partially loaded" this session* — there is a check, but no gate. C2 and C3 both flagged the over-cap; my contribution is the *contract diagnosis*: the cap is an advisory print, not a `--check`-style gate, and that is the entire reason it can drift past the ceiling.
- **The SessionStart roadmap freshness audit** (`tools/roadmap-audit/session-start.sh`) is *soft-deterministic*: the **check** is deterministic and well-engineered (hash equality with a real §12.2.1 fixed-point carve-out + a behind-origin guard), but the **enforcement** is a prose instruction to *Claude* (CLAUDE.md §12.3 "do NOT make substantive edits until operator response"). It emits `[ROADMAP DRIFT] ... action=§12.3`; the halt is model-discretion, not a blocking gate. Good check, no gate-teeth.

**Tier 3 — UNGATED (governed by prose only; no check at all).**
- **CLAUDE.md** has *no byte-budget gate, no schema, no freshness check* — it is governed entirely by the prose discipline *written inside CLAUDE.md itself* (§12.5.1 hygiene rules, the `optimize-claude-md` skill). C2's P4 (give CLAUDE.md an automated byte-budget mirroring the MEMORY.md cap precedent) is correct — but I sharpen it: **the MEMORY.md cap is the wrong precedent to mirror, because the MEMORY.md cap is itself only advisory.** Mirroring an advisory check yields a second advisory check. The right precedent is `substitution_ledger.py --check` — a `--check` that exits 1.
- **The `.harness/` clearance markers** are the cleanest "contract-without-a-gate" instance in the entire repo. `TEMPLATE.md` defines a real **frontmatter schema** — a typed `clearance_type` enum, required `artifact`/`version`/`merge_commit`/`reviewer_chain` fields. **Nothing validates a marker against that schema.** The conformance contract is *authored*; the deterministic gate that would enforce it does *not exist*. CLAUDE.md §4.5 even concedes the convention is "currently advisory at v1." A malformed marker, a wrong `clearance_type`, or a version-binding that doesn't match the cited artifact passes silently.
