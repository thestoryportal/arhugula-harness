## C7 — Observability: is the repo's own context/memory state legible to agent + operator?

### The frame (in-lane): the hooks are the *runtime-introspection layer over the repo's own context/memory state* — and they apply MY structure-not-content discipline

My domain spec anchor is **C-IS-07 §7.2** (the C2-pole read contract — the read half of the T-perm-2 seam C2 and C3 both led with). It mandates that durable state enters context **Navigation-primitive-mediated** and **Read-into-dynamic-suffix**. C2 read this as a *placement/budget* contract (correct, in C2's lane). I read the same seam through my lane: a navigation-primitive-mediated read is only safe if the *act of reading* — and the *state being read into* — is **observable**. C7 owns the question no other voice owns here: *of the context/memory state this repo carries across a session, what is made legible — to the agent at session-start, to the agent mid-session, and to the operator — and what stays silent?*

The load-bearing C7 finding is that the harness already has a **runtime-introspection layer for its own context/memory state — the 42-hook lifecycle** — and that layer embodies my permanent **structure-not-content discipline** (skill §4.5 / line 321) applied to the *repo's* context rather than to H_T's product runtime. Every legibility signal the hooks emit is **structural, never content**: `[ROADMAP] hash=ok next=… in_flight=N forks=N` at SessionStart; `[roadmap] next=… [possible drift]` on *every* UserPromptSubmit; `context_window.used_percentage` at the StatusLine (the *one* place context-fill is exposed to a script — verified at `context-recovery.sh:23`, and it drives a proactive checkpoint, a genuine telemetry→action loop); the `MEMORY.md NNNN B > 24400 B cap` flag; the `EVENT:TOOL:ERRTYPE` failure signature with cardinality-≥2 recurrence detection at `capture-failure.sh`. This is exactly the structure-not-content posture — structural attributes always-on, content never default-captured. The harness *can* do disciplined, bounded, structural runtime introspection of its own state, and does, at the hook layer. [HIGH — all signals read at source this session.]

So my consultant role is a **legibility audit**, not a re-statement of C2's read-cost or C3's storage findings. The discriminating C7 deliverable is the coverage asymmetry below.

That asymmetry IS the C7 finding. Five of the highest-value remediation targets C2 and C3 named are precisely the states with **zero legibility today** — you cannot manage what you cannot see, and the PLAN's first obligation for each is an *observability requirement*, logically prior to C3's retention policy or C2's cap value.

### Reactions to the primaries (concrete, non-formulaic)

**C2's P4 (CLAUDE.md cap + surfacing hook) — concur, but sharpen on *placement*.** This is the strongest single legibility add in the layer and the MEMORY.md cap is the working precedent C2 rightly cites. My in-lane refinement: the existing MEMORY.md cap fires *only at session boundaries* (loop-gc at SessionStart, session-end-cleanup at SessionEnd) — **never mid-session**. An agent that bloats CLAUDE.md (or MEMORY.md) *during* a long session never sees the flag until it's too late to act in that session. C7 owns *where the signal fires*; C2 owns the cap value. **PLAN requirement: byte-budget/health signals need a mid-session surface (UserPromptSubmit injector or StatusLine), not only boundaries.** This is the same gap the coverage map shows for MEMORY.md itself.

**C2's eval-ability pre-check — a genuine interaction I verified rather than asserted.** I checked whether cache-hit telemetry is reachable from *any* hook surface. It is **not**: the only token signal in the dev loop is `capture-failure.sh` logging `max_output_tokens` as a *failure* (StopFailure), and StatusLine exposes only `context_window.used_percentage`. Cache attribution (`gen_ai.usage.cache_creation.input_tokens` / `cache_read.input_tokens`) lives in **H_T product OTel** per cluster-4 §2.1 line 13 — it is not in Claude Code's hook JSON. **Therefore C2's proposed eval signal (b) "static-prefix cache-hit-rate on warm sessions" has no observability home in the dev loop** — flag it as a constraint, not a measurable. The good news, also verified: C2's signal (d) "§2-cell-edit-frequency per PR" *is* git-derivable (`git log -- CLAUDE.md` = 37 of last 60 commits touch CLAUDE.md — which both confirms C2's cache-detonation mechanism empirically AND gives the cost-proxy a real observability home). So the PLAN's cache-efficiency story should be measured by the **git-derivable edit-cadence proxy** (signal d), not by an unreachable cache-hit-rate (signal b).

**C3's retention findings (ledger bloat / checkpoint asymmetry / FM-H) — concur, with the legibility complement.** For each storage finding C3 owns the *policy* (`ledger_retention_days`, keep-N) and the *mechanism* (flock/lease). C7's complement: *before* you set a retention policy, there is **no observability of the growth or the collision** — you cannot watch the ledger accrete, and FM-H is "silent by construction" (C3's own phrase, and exactly right — a cross-worktree lost-update emits nothing). **PLAN requirement: each of the three states needs a health surface (ledger active-doc count vs archived; per-store checkpoint count vs ceiling; a MEMORY.md write-collision detector — e.g. a content-hash mismatch flag at the serialization point).** I own "is it observable"; I explicitly leave the policy and the serialization mechanism to C3.
