# C11 — Operator-Loop / Local-Deployment voice contribution

**Topic:** R-FS-1 arc B2 (multi-server MCP, DESIGN leg) — the `AS-MCPServerTrustLevel → CP-MCPTrustTier` projection (today a vacuous stub).

**Concern #6 pre-check (C11 sole owner, s14 §8):** (a) operator surface engaged — the per-server `trust_level` declaration the operator already authors at MCP registration; (b) local-deployment specifics — the solo-developer / local stdio out-of-box case; (c) does it compose with the thin-default-with-escalation posture, or broaden operator burden? — this contribution argues identity-by-ordinal is the *only* reading that doesn't broaden burden.

---

## (1) C11 position

**Identity-by-ordinal — faithfully project the operator's declared per-server `trust_level` (L0→LEVEL_0 … L3→LEVEL_3) and nothing more. No transport-aware clamps, no extra required attestation fields, no refuse-by-default inside the projection.** From the operator-loop / local-deployment / minimal-config-burden lens this is not a close call: the projection is a *value-set identity* between two enums that are the same four levels (the CP enum docstring itself calls `MCPTrustTier` a "byte-exact factor-out of the AS-owned value set" at `Spec_Action_Surface_v1.md` C-AS-10 §10.3). Putting a *trust decision* into an identity mapping both (i) silently overrides the config the operator already paid to declare, and (ii) duplicates enforcement that already lives — correctly — in a separate, fully-built mechanism. C11 commits identity-by-ordinal plainly; I do not manufacture a tension where the minimal honest design is obvious. [HIGH]

## (2) Reasoning — operator-burden + local-first

**The live bug is the argument.** Today's stub (`mcp_client_host_factory.py:197` `_trust_tier_from_level`) ignores its input and always returns `MCPTrustTier.LEVEL_0_REFUSE_REMOTE`. The consequence — body-verified — is that a solo developer who declared their *local stdio* MCP server as `L3_ALLOW_WITH_AUDIT` is gate-projected to refuse-remote anyway. That is the exact "surprise override" the operator-loop lens exists to prevent: the operator authored an intentional config and the harness silently substituted a different one. Honest config / no-surprise-override is a first-order C11 value, the local-deployment sibling of the verbatim-by-default operator-response-capture posture (s14 §4.1.9 — *the operator authored it intentionally; don't silently transform it*). Identity-by-ordinal is what makes the operator's declaration mean what it says.

**The operator has already paid the per-server cost.** `MCPClientConfig.trust_level: MCPServerTrustLevel` has **no default** (`harness-runtime/src/harness_runtime/types.py:592`) — it is a required field the operator declares per server. The thin-default-with-escalation posture (s14 §4.1 family A; FM-F) says burden must be justified by what it buys. Identity-by-ordinal means the burden the operator *already bears* buys the one thing it should: the declared level is honored end-to-end. Every in-projection clamp inverts this into the worst-of-both-worlds — the operator pays the declaration cost **and** gets overridden. Adding *further* required attestation fields on top of an already-required field is a textbook FM-F gate-fatigue-producer: per-server config ceremony imposed without a trigger that justifies it, on the most common out-of-box surface (local stdio, solo-developer, local-development-first per ADR-D2 3-tier).

**One source of truth.** The operator's declared per-server `trust_level` is the *source* of the trust intent; the dispatch-time gate is the *enforcement*. A projection that re-decides trust makes the projection a second authority over a value the operator already owns — a one-source-of-truth violation. Keep the projection a pure value-set identity; let trust decisions live at the layer that owns them.

## (3) What I would PUSH BACK on

I push back — from the C11 minimal-burden lens — on **every** form of in-projection ceremony the action-safety lens might reach for, with each rebuttal grounded in a mechanism that already exists at the right layer:

- **Transport-aware clamps inside the projection** (e.g. "force a lower tier when transport is remote"). PUSH BACK. This is redundant: the per-MCP-transport sandbox-tier *floor* already enters the gate-level `max()` composition at `Spec_Action_Surface_v1.md` C-AS-12 §12.1 (AS C-AS-10 §10.1 / §10.3) — transport severity is already priced in, at the floor, where it belongs. Duplicating it in the projection would be a second, divergent transport policy. (I am careful here: *which* transports warrant *which* floor is C10/AS discipline, not mine — C11's point is only that the burden is already absorbed elsewhere, so the projection needn't carry it.)
- **Extra required per-server attestation fields** (signatures, pins, egress allow-lists threaded into the projection contract). PUSH BACK. AS §10.3 already assigns those to the *registration* surface per level (Level 1 signature+pin, Level 2 sandbox+egress-allow-list, Level 3 per-call audit), explicitly "deferred to implementation discretion" at registration — not to the projection. Hoisting them into the projection adds required config to the hot path on top of an already-required field: FM-F gate-fatigue, no trigger justification.
- **Refuse-by-default inside the projection.** PUSH BACK hardest — this is *both* failure modes at once. (i) It is precisely what the stub does now, i.e. the bug (fact 3): it discards the operator's declaration. (ii) It is redundant with a conservative default that *already exists at the right layer* — `_default_tier_resolver` in `per_server_trust_evaluator.py` already returns `LEVEL_0_REFUSE_REMOTE` for an *unknown* server (the MIN-of-`MCPTrustTier`-members rule, evaluator docstring lines 27 + 88). The genuinely-dangerous "no declared trust" case is already refuse-defaulted where it should be. Re-imposing refuse-by-default on the *declared* case in the projection only re-creates the override bug.

**Engaging C10's likely concern directly** — *"identity-mapping lets an over-permissive declaration flow through unchecked."* Three precise rebuttals, none of which require ceremony in the projection: (1) it does **not** flow unchecked — every dispatch hits C-CP-27's `PerServerTrustEvaluator` 4-branch gate (deny-list wins over allow-list, per-server overrides, conservative tier-derivation, audit-floor; body-verified, fully built, multi-server-ready) **plus** the AS sandbox-tier floor's `max()` at C-AS-12 §12.1; (2) the truly unknown / undeclared server — the case worth fearing — is **already** refuse-defaulted at the evaluator's `_default_tier_resolver`; (3) the projection is a value-set identity, not a trust decision — siting the trust decision there would split authority over a value the operator owns. So C10's concern is real and *already answered* by mechanisms that exist; it does not motivate ceremony in the projection.

## (4) Where a safety concern legitimately belongs

Not in the projection. It belongs at the two separate defense-in-depth mechanisms that already own it and are already multi-server-ready:

1. **`PerServerTrustEvaluator` (CP C-CP-27 §27, `harness-cp/src/harness_cp/per_server_trust_evaluator.py`)** — the dispatch-time gate: deny-wins-over-allow 4-branch dispatch, per-server overrides, conservative-MIN tier-derivation for unknown servers, audit-floor. This is the right home for "is this server's declared level safe to act on, right now, for this call." C-CP-27 is `PRESERVED VERBATIM` at the current spec head (`Spec_Control_Plane_v1_33.md` §-filing-footer table).
2. **The per-MCP-transport sandbox-tier floor (AS C-AS-10 §10.1 / §10.3 → `max()` at C-AS-12 §12.1)** — the containment floor that prices in transport severity independent of the declared level.

The discipline boundary I am explicitly honoring (s14 §7.10; FM-C; "MCP trust tier" is named C10's discipline, *not* C11's): I do **not** author trigger conditions or trust-tier policy — C11 owns the *operator-burden cost axis* of the C4↔C10 tradeoff (`per_mcp_server_trust_tier`, s14 §7.11), and from that axis the call is unambiguous. The trust *decision* and its triggers are C10's; the *enforcement* is C-CP-27 + the AS floor; the *projection* should stay a faithful identity. If the action-safety lens believes the conservative default for *undeclared* servers should be stricter, that is a change to the evaluator's `_default_tier_resolver` / C-CP-27 discipline (C10's surface) — **not** a reason to clamp the *declared* case in the projection.

From the operator-loop / local-deployment / minimal-burden lens, identity-by-ordinal is the minimal honest design and the only reading that does not silently override the operator's declared config.
