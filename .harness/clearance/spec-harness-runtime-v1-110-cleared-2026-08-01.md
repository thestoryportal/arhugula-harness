# Clearance — `Spec Harness Runtime v1.110`

v1.110 carries **TWO amendment sites, both pure DECLARATIONS of an EXISTING limit**: a new closing paragraph at **§14.14.9.1** (the durable-pause-state read accessor) and a new invariant bullet in **§30**'s list, immediately after `Durable-handle read (v1.46)`. Both state, symmetrically and in the terms §13.7 term **3-bis** already uses for the operator enumeration, that the store is **append-only, never truncated, and writes NO pause-resolved marker**, so a journal whose pause was successfully resumed is **byte-indistinguishable** from one whose pause is still outstanding — the read reports the **latest durable record**, which is **not a liveness claim**.

**The limit is DECLARED, not FIXED.** No shipped behaviour changes: the accessor returns exactly what it returned at v1.109 and `resume()` admits exactly what it admitted at v1.109. **NO new `C-RT-*` number** (a doc-only declaration leg cannot mint one), **NO carrier retyped, NO field added, NO `snapshot_hash` impact, NO cause-vocabulary change** (five members, still CLOSED), **NO plan unit owed** at either the Runtime or the OD plan, and **NO CXA row** (aggregate frozen at 111 — this delta introduces no cross-package consumption at all). §13.7 / §13.7.1, §14.14.8, §14.14.9.2 – §14.14.9.6, §30's cause table and the entire §30 failure-mode taxonomy are **PRESERVED VERBATIM**.

Reviewed at clearance: that the declaration's scope is the **steady state**, not merely the pre-v1.108 legacy set (the fork's §3(vii) ground); that the §30 bullet does not overstate — `resume()`'s `snapshot_hash` / workflow-match / step-index-range guards validate integrity and applicability, never outstandingness, and the §30 staleness precondition fences a composed claim against a **changed** record, so a re-resume of an **unchanged, already-resolved** record is admitted without refusal; and that the deferred half is recorded as a **falsifiable** four-disjunct demand test (D-0…D-3) on the register row rather than as an open-ended "if the operator loop demands it".
