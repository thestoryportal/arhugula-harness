## 1. Charter (verbatim authority)

CP spec `Spec_Control_Plane_v1_91.md` item 6 registers exactly two findings, deliberately out-of-scope at v1.91 because "their correct dispositions carry open semantic questions (notably store-capture-or-not for a cross-attempt fence peer) that belong to their own design pass":

> (a) **Fence-ABORT terminal exit bypass** — the tier-agnostic `parallelization-effect-fence-aborted` FAILED return fires BEFORE every scan site (pre-existing exit shape, unchanged on main). Under warm-up, a Phase-1 `EffectFenceAbortedError` withholds all siblings and this exit records zero footprint for the aborted branch and the withheld siblings. Naively running the scan there would mislabel the ABORTED branch `cancelled` (it DID dispatch — the fence arm keys on the *paused* dict, not the *aborted* set), so inclusion requires its own disposition mapping.
>
> (b) **Snapshot-carried fence-peer asymmetry** — the scan's fence arm consults this-round `effect_fence_paused_dispositions` only, not the snapshot-carried `_recovered_effect_fence_paused`. A previously-fence-paused peer re-dispatched on a resume round and withheld by a Phase-1 deadline strike synthesizes `cancelled`, which is false cross-attempt (its prior dispatch fired; its store marker + fence state remain honest — audit-surface only).

Bounding (same item): "Both are bounded to compound fence × warm-up × deadline paths; store markers, snapshots, resume, and crash-resume stay honest throughout (review-confirmed)." This arc is **audit-surface-only**: ledger terminal records (and, if ratified, store captures) at terminal exits. No dispatch-behavior change, no resume-behavior change.

### 2.1 The warm-up composition that makes both findings reachable

Under `concurrent_cache_warmup` (default ON since v1.89), Phase 1 serializes `branch[0]`; a Phase-1 `EffectFenceAbortedError` (finding a) or Phase-1 deadline strike (finding b) withholds ALL of branches[1..N-1]. Both findings pre-exist warm-up in narrower shapes (any ABORT collapse withholds not-yet-scheduled TaskGroup siblings), but warm-up makes "all siblings withheld, zero footprint" the systematic outcome — the same amplification that motivated M2 at v1.91.

## 4. Design questions

- **Q1 (finding a).** Should the fence-ABORT exit run the scan, and with what disposition for the ABORTED branch (class A1)? Constraint: ledger vocabulary is `{cancelled, completed, timed_out}` — adding an enum value would be an IS-hash / schema change (out of audit-surface-only scope). Constraint: store capture for the aborted branch must stay within `{completed, timed_out, scoped_aborted}` or not be written at all.
- **Q2 (finding b, and class A3/A4 at the ABORT exit).** Should the scan's fence arm consult `_recovered_effect_fence_paused` in addition to this-round `effect_fence_paused_dispositions`? If yes, what does a recovered (cross-attempt) fence peer record — and does it get the store capture (the named open semantic question)? Tension: a store `completed` capture is what makes crash-resume refuse to re-dispatch (the Codex [P2] rationale for this-round peers) — but the recovered peer's resolvability on a later stale-snapshot resume flows from the SNAPSHOT (which still carries it) and the reserve (at-most-once safety), so a capture may be (i) unnecessary, (ii) harmful (if any resume path reads captures as skip-authority), or (iii) required for crash-during-drain honesty — to be pinned by FACT 4 (does operator resume consult the store?).

## 5. Facts pinned before optioning (gathered at arc-open; verbatim-quote grounding pass, all re-checkable at HEAD)

**F1 — the scoped-abort recording convention** (`workflow_driver.py:7317-7345` + CP spec v1.74 §1). An `ABORT_BRANCH` ordinal records **ledger terminal `completed`** (`append_branch_terminal_ledger_entry(..., terminal_status="completed")` :7321) and **store `scoped_aborted`** (`_capture_branch_terminal(..., terminal_status="scoped_aborted")` :7341). The inline comment names the split: the IS-hash-bearing F2 LEDGER append stays `completed` → no §5.2 IS-hash change; **only the runtime store carries the distinguishing value**, and it exists specifically so crash-mid-resume reconstructs `_scoped_abort_ordinals` (the seed loop :7206-7219 re-adds `scoped_aborted` store terminals) and reproduces the scoped-abort FAILED fold across a crash.

**F2 — LINEAR analogue.** The LINEAR path has NO `EffectFenceAbortedError`-specific handler — it falls to the generic step-failure return (:4593-4601, `RunStatus.FAILED` + `_step_fail_class("step-failure", exc)`); the aborted step is recorded like any failed step. LINEAR has no zero-footprint abort gap; the PARALLELIZATION ABORT exit's silence is the outlier being fixed.

**F3 — committed disposition vocabulary** (Spec_Control_Plane_v1_32.md §25.15.2): obligation 4 defines `cancelled` ⟹ "terminated at a **not-yet-dispatched** boundary (no effectful dispatch at the termination point)"; `completed` / `timed_out` ⟹ "the branch's in-flight step ran (**effect may have landed**) and is recorded". Obligation 3 (audit-completeness) targets "no silent **landed** effect". Obligation 7: resume MUST NOT re-dispatch a branch persisted `cancelled`/`completed`/`timed_out` — but per v1.91 item 5 (committed), synthesized ledger terminals are per-attempt AUDIT records and re-dispatch eligibility keys on **snapshot omission**, not ledger reads. v1.65 §1 additionally commits: "Under `CascadePolicy.CASCADE_CANCEL` an effect-fence-paused branch is recorded `completed` (dispatch-boundary — its effect may have landed; it MUST NOT be mislabeled `cancelled`)" and "ABORT is terminal … the driver forces `RunStatus.FAILED` tier-agnostically". v1.73 §1 commits the `ABORT_BRANCH` recording shape quoted at F1 — **including ledger `completed` for a branch that never re-dispatched this round** ("EXCLUDED from the re-dispatch plan … recorded a `completed`/no-output terminal").

**F4 — resume skip-set authority.** On operator resume, the skip-set is derived ONLY from the snapshot's `branches` tuple (`_recovered_terminal` :6783; branch_plan filter `if branch_index in _recovered_terminal: continue` :7002). Store captures do NOT feed the operator-resume skip-set; their sole consumer is **crash-resume** classification (store terminal present → recovered-terminal; marker-present + no terminal → MAYBE-RAN → fail-closed / fence-recoverable reconstruct).

## 6. Decision (per question), with the reasoning that binds it

### Q1 — the fence-ABORT exit: RUN the scan there, with a new aborted arm; ledger `completed` terminal-ONLY for the ABORTED branch; NO step entry; NO store capture.

- **Scan inclusion.** Add the fifth scan call site immediately before the `parallelization-effect-fence-aborted` return (:7993-7996), so withheld siblings (classes A2/A3/A4) stop leaving zero footprint — the exact M2 rationale, applied to the exit M2 deliberately skipped. `fail_class` and run status unchanged.
- **Aborted-arm disposition = ledger `completed`, terminal-only.** The ledger vocabulary has no "aborted" (F-map §2); `cancelled` is false (its dispatch fired — v1.91 item 6a); `timed_out` is false (nothing deadline-cut it). `completed` is the committed dispatch-boundary value for the entire abort/fence family (F1: ABORT_BRANCH → ledger `completed`; F3: fence-paused-at-CASCADE_CANCEL → `completed` "MUST NOT be mislabeled `cancelled`"). The abort semantics stay carried by the run-level `fail_class` + the durable fence claim (:7991-7992) — the same "distinguishing value lives elsewhere" split F1 committed.
- **NO step entry.** Obligation 3 exists for landed effects; an ABORT is the fence *refusing* the effect — provably nothing landed. The scan's committed terminal-ONLY discipline (v1.91 item 3: "a step entry would claim a dispatch that never fired" — here: would claim an effectful *step* ran when the fence refused it) holds for every arm. Precedent: ABORT_BRANCH records no step entry (F1).
- **NO store capture.** Today the aborted branch's store state is marker-present + no terminal (marked at :7730 pre-dispatch) → crash-mid-drain classifies MAYBE-RAN → fail-closed / fence-recoverable — honest (the fence claim refuses any re-fire; ABORT-refusal is idempotent). A `completed` capture would flip crash-resume to recovered-terminal and let a crashed abort round complete as if the ABORT hadn't failed the run — a *behavior* change that would need its own F1-style fold-reconstruction machinery (the `scoped_aborted` precedent), which is exactly what this audit-surface-only arc must not build. Byte-preserving the store preserves crash semantics byte-exact.
- **Recording site: in the scan (aborted arm), not at the catch sites.** The catches (:7761-7765, :7843-7849) stay minimal (set-add + re-raise, as today). Rationale: the record is terminal-only (the scan's committed discipline), the scan is the single audited synthesis surface, and the aborted branch reaches it naturally (it is in `branch_plan`; `_writer_has_branch_disposition` is false for it). Arm ORDER is load-bearing: aborted-arm FIRST (an aborted ordinal also appears in `_recovered_effect_fence_paused`), then fence-paused-union, then `cancelled`.

### Q2 — the fence arm consults the UNION of this-round `effect_fence_paused_dispositions` and snapshot-carried `_recovered_effect_fence_paused`; a recovered-withheld peer records ledger `completed` with **NO store capture** (the named open question answered: capture-NOT).

- **Ledger `completed` is the honest obligation-4 value.** F3's committed definition — `completed` ⟹ "effect may have landed" — is *literally* the recovered peer's state: attempt-1's dispatch fired and holds an ambiguous-uncommitted reserve. `cancelled` ("no effectful dispatch at the termination point") contradicts the branch's own store (marker present, reserve held) — the defect under repair. F3's ABORT_BRANCH precedent already commits ledger `completed` for a fence peer that never re-dispatched this round, so the cross-attempt reading of `completed` is convention-committed, not novel.
- **NO store capture — the snapshot-carried asymmetry is the load-bearing justification.** The v1.91 item-3 capture for THIS-round peers exists because a terminal exit captures no snapshot: without the store record, a crash would mis-classify the peer (Codex [P2]) — the store is the only durable carrier. A RECOVERED peer is different in exactly one durable respect: the prior round's still-journaled snapshot ALREADY carries it in `effect_fence_paused_branches`. Its crash-resume classification today — marker-present + no store terminal → fence-recoverable MAYBE-RAN → reconstruct re-pauses it, resolvable — is the honest outcome for a still-unresolved reserve, and F4 confirms operator stale-resume resolvability flows from the snapshot alone. Writing a capture would flip its crash class to recovered-terminal, silently destroying the peer's crash-path resolvability — a behavior change. Capture-NOT keeps store/snapshot/resume/crash-resume byte-identical (the arc's §8 bounding) while fixing the ledger claim.
- **This-round peers keep the v1.91 committed treatment** (`completed` + capture) — including an INERT re-paused peer on a resume round (it is in the this-round dict). Inherited-committed at the new ABORT exit call site, not redesigned here. **Named consequence (review finding 1, accepted):** at the NEW exit this inherited capture is a crash-visible addition — today an A4 peer at a crashed ABORT round classifies fence-recoverable maybe-ran (resolvable); post-arc its capture makes it recovered-terminal and flips `_crash_pause_trigger` (:3185-3188). This is the committed v1.65 §1(c) trade (reproduce-the-terminal over preserve-resolvability) extended to one more exit — deliberate, named in the v1.92 delta, pinned by FL4's store assertion. The byte-identical crash-resume claim is therefore scoped to the two CAPTURE-LESS arms (aborted branch + recovered-withheld peer), not the arc as a whole.

### Q3 (scope) — ORCHESTRATOR_WORKERS: REGISTER `B-18-FENCE-LEDGER-FIDELITY-OW` as its own follow-on; do not widen this arc.

Reviewer grounding (review check 7): O-W **shares** the zero-footprint ABORT-exit shape (:10772-10777 fires before its scan) — but symmetric inclusion is NOT two mechanical edits: (a) O-W's scan is an inline CASCADE_CANCEL-only loop (:10787-10828) — its deadline and pause-tier exits have NO scan at all (the M2 counterpart never landed for O-W; v1.91 item 2 is explicitly PARALLELIZATION-only); (b) O-W carries a third disposition class (`paused_child_dispositions` union :10804); (c) O-W has its own recovered dict (:9128). The honest O-W follow-on scope = the aborted-return bypass + the never-landed M2 terminal exits + the union arm, against O-W's three-class arms. Registered in this PR's arc-ledger edit with that scope.
